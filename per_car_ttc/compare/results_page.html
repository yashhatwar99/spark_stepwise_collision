BTP · single-camera collision prediction

Per-car TTC Results

Three ways to turn FP-TTC's output into one time to collision per car, scored on the same cars. Lower error is better. "Cars under 3 s found" is the share of cars that really are under 3 s from colliding that the method also flagged.

The median wins. On nuScenes, data FP-TTC never saw, it is off by 0.94 s in the middle and finds 87% of the cars that really are under 3 s from a collision.

The learned head ties it on the 85 held-out KITTI cars (0.27 s, the same as the median), but on nuScenes it drops to 1.61 s and 57%. The flow + affine zoom does not work yet: 5.29 s and 23%.

Time

On the laptop's RTX 3050, one frame pair at 640×320: YOLO 12 ms, FP-TTC 316 ms (the model run is almost all of the time), the median for every car in the frame 0.3 ms, the learned head about 0.4 ms per car. In total about 328 ms, 3.0 frames per second. The per-car step is not what costs time.

Read these numbers with care

  • TTC is the paper's formula, Δt ÷ (1 − s), with s the model's scale output and Δt the time between the frames. It assumes the closing speed stays constant.
  • The 3 s danger line is our own choice. The paper scores per pixel at 1, 2 and 5 s.
  • The nuScenes truth is calculated, from labelled positions and speeds. It is not a recorded crash. A test on real crash times (CCD) is still to do.
  • FP-TTC was fine-tuned on KITTI, so KITTI alone is not a clean test. nuScenes is.
  • The head was trained on 299 KITTI cars from one camera. Why it falls behind on nuScenes is not tested. Likely causes are too few cars and the different camera and picture shape.
  • Flow + affine zoom fails for a reason not yet found. The flow FP-TTC returns is far from KITTI's true flow, so the fitted zoom is wrong.
  • Not measured here: the light model (the paper describes it, but it has no released weights).
