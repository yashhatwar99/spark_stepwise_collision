KITTI Per-car Results

The Median method and the Learned head, run on all 200 KITTI training pairs and compared with the true TTC from KITTI's measured depth. Lower error is better.

The median is closer to the true TTC. Over all images it is off by 0.21 s in the middle, against 0.52 s for the learned head. On the 40 held-out images, which neither the model nor the head learned from, it is 0.37 s against 0.72 s.

The median also finds more of the really dangerous cars (true TTC under 3 s): 94% against 86% over all images, and 93% against 81% on the held-out ones.

Read these numbers with care

  • Boxes come from YOLO, not from KITTI's car outlines. A box can include road or a second car, so the true TTC inside it is a median of what KITTI measured there, not one car's exact value.
  • Only cars with enough measured pixels have a truth. Far and small cars often do not, so they are left out of the error numbers.
  • FP-TTC learned from 160 of the 200 pairs. The 40 held-out images (numbers divisible by 5) are the fair test. The head learned from 140 of the other 160 images and used the other 20 to pick its best round.
  • TTC is the paper's formula, Δt ÷ (1 − s) with Δt = 0.1 s, and assumes constant speed.
  • No crashes are involved. KITTI is normal driving, so the true TTC is a calculation from measured depth, not a recorded collision.
  • nuScenes is not on this page. The head fell further behind there.
